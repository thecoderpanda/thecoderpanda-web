# Why Developer Relations Is the Hottest Job in Tech Right Now

> **TL;DR:** The traditional enterprise sales playbook is dead. Developers are the new decision-makers in tech, and they have an absolute allergy to corporate marketing. This power shift has created a massive, lucrative boom in Developer Relations (DevRel)—a hybrid role that requires solid engineering chops, product empathy, and the rare ability to communicate with actual humans.

For decades, selling software to enterprises was a highly structured, remarkably boring game. An enterprise software sales representative in an expensive, slightly ill-fitting suit would take a corporate Chief Technology Officer (CTO) out for an expensive steak dinner, play 18 holes of golf on a Tuesday morning, and hand over a glossy brochure filled with buzzwords like "synergy," "enterprise-grade compliance," and "end-to-end digital transformation." A multi-million dollar license agreement would be signed, and the software would be shoved down the throats of the engineering team, who would spend the next three years crying into their keyboards trying to configure it.

But in 2019, that playbook is completely obsolete. If you try to sell a developer-facing tool to a modern engineering team using that approach, you’ll be laughed out of the room. Developers don't read glossy brochures. They don't care about your sales representative's golf handicap. In fact, if a marketing email uses the word "revolutionary" or "seamless" more than once, a developer will block your entire domain, write an angry post on Reddit, and build an open-source alternative in Go over the weekend just out of pure spite. 

Developers are the ultimate kingmakers in tech today, and their rise has spawned one of the fastest-growing, highest-paying, and most misunderstood professions in the industry: Developer Relations (DevRel).

## The Rise of the Developer-Buyer

To understand why DevRel is suddenly so critical, you have to understand how purchasing decisions are made in modern organizations. The top-down sales model has been replaced by a bottoms-up, developer-first adoption cycle. When a team needs to solve a problem—say, sending transactional SMS messages, processing credit cards, or setting up cloud infrastructure—they don't wait for a formal procurement review. An individual engineer goes to Google, searches for a solution, finds a developer portal, copies an API key, runs `npm install`, and tests the integration in five minutes.

If it works, they drag it into their codebase. By the time the CTO or the procurement department even hears about the software, it is already integrated into the production pipeline, handling critical traffic, and the company is happily paying for it with a corporate credit card. 

Companies like Stripe, Twilio, and GitHub didn’t build multi-billion dollar businesses by pitching executives. They built them by making individual developers fall in love with their products. In this new world, the traditional sales representative is useless. You cannot pitch an API using slides; you have to pitch it using code, documentation, and tooling. And that is where Developer Advocates and Developer Relations managers come in.

## What DevRel Actually Is (And What It Isn't)

There is a common, highly cynical misconception that Developer Relations is simply marketing with a GitHub account. Critics think DevRel is a cushy job where you fly around the world on the company's dime, hang out at conferences, get slightly tipsy on craft beer, and hand out branded socks, t-shirts, and stickers to anyone who walks past your booth.

While it's true that DevRel involves a fair amount of community engagement (and yes, excellent swag), the actual role is highly technical and strategically vital. A great developer advocate is, first and foremost, a solid engineer. You cannot advocate for developers if you don't understand their pain. You need to be able to write production-grade sample applications, debug weird integration errors on stage, write comprehensive technical tutorials, and contribute directly to open-source libraries.

More importantly, DevRel is a bilingual translation role. You are a bridge. You speak fluent JSON to the community, and you speak fluent product roadmap and business metrics to your internal team. When developers are struggling with your SDK, you are their champion, bringing their brutal feedback directly to the product managers to ensure the issues get fixed. When your internal product team releases a new feature, you are the one translating complex technical changes into clear, exciting, and actionable guides that help developers succeed.

## Why Developers Hate Marketing (And Why DevRel Works)

Developers possess a finely tuned, highly sensitive bullshit detector. They have been burned too many times by companies making grand promises that their software cannot deliver. They know that a glossy marketing page is usually hiding a buggy, undocumented, and frustrating product.

DevRel works because it completely bypasses traditional marketing tactics and communicates with developers on their own terms. It is "engineering as marketing." Instead of showing a developer a stock photo of happy businesspeople, a developer advocate shows them an open GitHub repository. Instead of giving them a sales pitch, you give them a working demo. 

DevRel is rooted in education, utility, and respect. You win the developer's mind by helping them solve their immediate, real-world problems. If you write an incredibly detailed tutorial that explains how to debug a complex asynchronous database query in Node.js, and that tutorial happens to use your company's database product as the example, the developer will love you. They won't feel like they've been marketed to; they'll feel like they've been taught. They will remember who helped them when they were stuck at 11 PM, and when it’s time to choose a database for their next project, your product will be the default choice.

## The Career Arbitrage of the Decade

For software engineers, DevRel represents one of the most exciting career paths in the industry. It is a classic arbitrage opportunity. There is a massive supply of engineers who can write code, and there is a massive supply of marketing professionals who can write copy. But the intersection of those two circles—people who can write elegant, robust code *and* write engaging articles, speak on stage, and build a vibrant community—is incredibly small.

If you are an engineer who enjoys coding but is starting to feel burned out by the endless sprint cycles, the daily standups, and the isolation of staring at Jira tickets all day, DevRel is your escape hatch. It allows you to leverage your technical skills while exercising your creativity, writing, and communication abilities.

And because these skills are so rare, companies are willing to pay an absolute premium for them. In 2019, senior developer advocates are regularly commanding salaries that are on par with or exceed those of senior software engineers, with the added benefit of traveling, building a massive personal brand, and having a direct impact on the technical ecosystem. It is a highly demanding, highly rewarding role, and as more companies realize that developer adoption is the key to business survival, the demand for DevRel is only going to skyrocket.

## Key Takeaways

- **Bottoms-Up Revolution**: Modern tech sales are driven by developers selecting tools on their own, making traditional top-down executive pitching ineffective.
- **The Core Bridge**: DevRel is a highly technical liaison role that communicates live user feedback to product teams and translates complex updates to the community.
- **Utility over Hype**: Engineers respond only to transparent, educational, and practical resources like open-source repos, detailed docs, and live code examples.
- **The Unicorn Skillset**: Combining deep technical expertise with strong interpersonal, writing, and public presentation skills commands premium compensation in 2019.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Do I need to be an expert coder to work in Developer Relations?**
A: Yes, you need a strong engineering baseline. If you cannot write clean, functional code and build complex integrations, developers will instantly spot the lack of depth, and you will lose all credibility with the community.

**Q: Is DevRel part of the marketing department or the engineering department?**
A: It varies by company, but the most successful DevRel teams are either embedded within the engineering department or operate as an independent department that collaborates closely with product, marketing, and sales.

**Q: How is the success of a DevRel program measured?**
A: Success is measured by metrics like API key signups, SDK downloads, documentation pageviews, active developer community size, GitHub repository stars/forks, and ultimately, self-serve revenue growth.
