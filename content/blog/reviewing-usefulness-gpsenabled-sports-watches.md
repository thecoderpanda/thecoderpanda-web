For just under a decade now, GPS technology has driven the technology behind navigation and satellite based positioning and communication. Today, it powers the cutting-edge piece of technology that fits snuggly on your wrist: the GPS sports-watch. These wrist-sized mini-computers run the gauntlet from simple GPS locators and speed calculators to a way to completely automate (the planning of) your workout routine. Indeed many GPS watches on the market offer features completely unrelated to sports. But sports watches are in high demand among athletes for their ability it to provide accurate metric and biometric information, while on the go. One can be bought for under hundred pounds.

![Leikr-GPS-Watch-](http://i0.wp.com/www.itvizkids.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/Leikr-GPS-Watch-.jpg?resize=500%2C446)

A GPS watch may not seem, at first glance, to provide you with a wealth of indispensable information. But look further and you will not fault a decent GPS watch for lacking features. GPS watches currently on the market may have several pages of information, all customizable and selectable, so that they appear on the home-screen. Some of these may include pace/speed related information, heart rate monitors, and cumulative distance measurements. Many runners watchers also have excellent lapping capabilities via gps, so it is possible to see how many laps have been completed, and related timing information.

Many watches are fine tuned to the needs of runners and trainers, even those that are on marathon training (battery life is always a concern then, but most wireless-usb powered battery packs can go in excess of twelve hours easily. Features like a backlit screen for nighttime running are common and show just how much most watch-making companies have jumped on the sport bandwagon to offer competent devices.

![pyxis-gps-watch](http://i0.wp.com/www.itvizkids.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/pyxis-gps-watch.jpg?resize=450%2C322)

Most models of runner’s sports watches also have a variant or two designed for multiples sports and triathletes. Features like being water-proof upto fifty-metres are fairly common, and information and metrics are tailored to swimming and biking as well. Some watches have attachable sensors for bikes, to measure speeds and distance based on wheels and independent of GPS. Features like vibrating and toned alerts upon completing laps for example, are available. Some watches have facilities to display both short-term and long-term goals, as completed percentages as well.

![1173563_7203_625x1000](http://i0.wp.com/www.itvizkids.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/1173563_7203_625x1000.jpg?resize=374%2C573)

Other sassy features like touchscreen are becoming more available and popular, as display technology gets better, for crisper lettering. Several add-on features are available to some models, like additional sensors, that provide better biometric information, when attached to the body. Accelerometers for example do not need gps to function, and therefore are sometimes useful to have when indoors, at a gym workout or treadmill run. Some devices can actually look very different to regular watches. However, not everybody likes a watch that stands out, and therefore models that are hard to differentiate from normal watches will always be available. As technology increases, GPS will be used in devices like these for a variety of other purposes. For now, the sports world, from weekend-joggers to professional athletes will always find some use to wear a GPS sports watch.
