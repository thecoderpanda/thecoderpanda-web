## Authenticity and Transparency in Leadership: The Importance of Being True to Yourself and Your Community:

After my transformative experience at the Google Developers Conference in Mumbai, I became determined to establish a developer community in my home city. However, before I could begin, I needed to gain a deeper understanding of how to go about building a community.

I started by reaching out to established community leaders and gathering insight into their experiences building and running developer communities. Through these conversations, I gained a general understanding of the process. Armed with this knowledge, I registered for a Google Developers Group chapter in my city, and in 2018, I established GDG in Nagpur with a team of three people.

However, my journey was not without its challenges. My initial attempts at hosting meetups, such as an introduction to Google technologies, were met with feelings of nervousness and self-doubt. I even had an instance where I froze during a presentation in front of a large audience. This experience was embarrassing and led to a period of inactivity within the community.

It wasn't until I came across a podcast featuring entrepreneur Kunal Shah, discussing his own struggles with public speaking and how he overcame them to build a successful company, that I found the motivation to push through my own fears. I turned to comedy as a way to make my presentations more engaging and relatable. Despite initial struggles, I found that incorporating humour and relatable visuals into my presentations helped to boost my confidence and improve my delivery.

As my community grew, I realized the importance of networking and building connections. I reached out to community leaders and organizers but struggled to make meaningful connections. It wasn't until I adopted a "Keep it Simple and Sweet" approach to communication that I was able to make progress in this area.

Through my journey, I learned that building a developer community requires a combination of technical understanding, effective communication, and the ability to connect with others. My experience has taught me that building a community is a continuous process of learning, growing and adapting, but the end results are worth all the effort.

## Developing a Leadership Identity: Strategies for Building a Strong and Effective Persona:

In today's technological landscape, understanding and staying up-to-date on emerging topics such as Blockchain, AI, and the Cloud is crucial for engaging and educating a community of developers. I recognized the importance of building technical competencies in order to effectively convey these topics to my community audience.

To achieve this, I dedicated a significant amount of time to reading and gathering information on a daily basis, spending at least 2 hours per day on websites and podcasts. Additionally, I built my online presence by creating profiles on platforms such as GitHub and LinkedIn, and actively participating in online repositories and communities.

I also prioritised continuously improving my skill set by setting weekly learning goals and sharing what I had learned with my community through presentations and workshops. The efforts paid off as I received positive feedback from community members, and even got to hear how the technical competencies helped them land jobs. Through our community activities and speaker sessions, many found inspiration to continue learning and developing their skills.

## Navigating Challenges through Mentorship: Gaining Insight and Direction from Experienced Leaders:

As a community builder, it is essential to have a source of inspiration to turn to when facing challenges. For me, this came in the form of connecting with experienced community leaders who could provide guidance and support.

Early on in my journey, I struggled with feeling disconnected from other community leaders and achieving the level of engagement I desired for my own community. It was through a chance encounter with the profile of a community manager at Google on LinkedIn that I was able to gain valuable insight and perspective on the potential of community building. This individual, through our conversations, helped me understand the importance of setting specific goals for a community, and provided a roadmap for achieving them.

It is important to remember that not every source of inspiration will be immediately accessible or responsive. In those instances, it can be helpful to turn to alternative sources such as books, videos, or blogs from respected authors to gain inspiration and perspective.

## Overcoming Obstacles: Strategies for Handling Rejection and Maintaining Perseverance

Rejection is an unfortunate but all-too-common reality in the pursuit of leadership and personal growth. However, it is important to remember that rejection can serve as a valuable learning opportunity, helping us to build resilience, perseverance and determination.

As I have experienced in my own journey, rejection has played a significant role in shaping my leadership identity. I encountered rejection during my attempts to pursue higher education in the United States and in my early career aspirations, including a failed application to Google for an SDE role. I faced similar challenges when organizing my first hackathon, as several sponsors backed out and I struggled to secure funding.

At these moments, I turned to the words of leaders such as Steve Jobs and his famous Stanford speech to find inspiration and motivation to push through these challenges. I learned the importance of connecting the dots, understanding that every action we take has a purpose, even if it is not immediately clear. I also realized that persistence and a willingness to learn and improve are key to overcoming rejection and building a strong leadership persona.

Through practising these strategies, I was able to successfully organize the hackathon with more than 80 project submissions, and even though I faced rejections from investors and mentors while running my startup, I kept working on my shortcomings, building a better network and skillsets, and a never-give-up attitude.

## Enhancing Leadership Through Action: The Importance of Practicing and Participating

After my experiences in building and leading developer communities, I had the opportunity to work with notable companies such as Caterpillar, Google India, and Eros International, as well as co-founding two startups. Eventually, I found myself drawn to the exciting possibilities of Web3 technology, particularly the concept of decentralization and user-controlled data.

To improve my skills in this area, I began learning and experimenting with Web3 technology on a daily basis. I also put my previous experience to use by building a startup that aimed to bring together AI and Blockchain, with the goal of rewarding AI enthusiasts for creating and hosting their own models. Unfortunately, despite raising initial funding, the startup was ultimately unsuccessful.

However, I did not let this setback discourage me. Along with my co-founder, I decided to participate in hackathons and other opportunities to continue developing our skills and exploring new ideas. It was through one such event, an ETHGlobal hackathon, that I first came across Covalent, and had the opportunity to build a basic asset-tracking app using the platform. Although we did not win any bounties, the experience was a valuable learning opportunity and led to my eventual involvement in the Covalent community.

## Embracing Risk: The Importance of Being Courageous and Taking a Leap

I had the opportunity to learn more about Covalent and its development while participating in a hackathon. Through researching the company and its team, I was impressed by the dedication and hard work of the team, particularly that of its leader, Ganesh. This motivated me to reach out to Ganesh via Twitter, expressing my interest in learning more about the company and potentially joining the team. To my delight, Ganesh responded positively and encouraged me to send my resume. Our initial conversation was a bit nerve-wracking for me, but it went well and soon after, I received an offer for a Developer Relations role at Covalent.

Since joining the company, I have been impressed by the supportive and collaborative culture at Covalent, and I am grateful for the opportunity to pursue my dream of building and leading developer communities. The support and guidance provided by Ganesh and Harish in this role have been invaluable.

## Advancing Knowledge Through Sharing: The Importance of Communicating What We Learn

Once I began my tenure with Covalent, my goal was to establish and lead an innovative meetup program to promote and educate about Web3 technology. As a relatively new and rapidly evolving field, it can be challenging to effectively communicate and spread awareness about the benefits and potential of Web3 technology.

Drawing from my previous experiences in building communities and startups, I developed a comprehensive program to bridge the knowledge gap and provide individuals and groups with the tools and resources needed to establish and grow their own Web3 communities. The program focuses on four main pillars: Learn, Educate, Evangelize, and Earn. Through a series of structured activities and resources, participants are able to deepen their understanding of Web3 technology, develop the skills to host their own meetups and events, and ultimately grow and support the Web3 ecosystem. The program culminates in the creation of a "meetup-in-a-box" solution, which provides a framework and guidance for individuals and groups to host their own successful Web3 meetups.

## Achieving Success through a Positive Mindset: Building Strong Communities and Programs

My goal with the Meetup-in-a-box (MIAB) program was to create an educational, intuitive, and rewarding community platform. With the support of my dedicated teammates, I am proud to have achieved this goal and successfully scaled the program to a global level. Initially, it was challenging to understand the varying levels of knowledge and comprehension of Web3 technology among different regions and communities. To address this, I began by hosting small test sessions to gauge audience engagement and comprehension. I then engaged in one-on-one conversations with community members to gain a deeper understanding of their knowledge and perspective towards Web3 technology. This helped me identify the key challenges and obstacles that individuals and groups faced when trying to establish and grow their own Web3 communities.

I began by focusing on my hometown of Nagpur, Maharashtra, India and leveraging my existing network through my involvement in GDG Nagpur (Google Developer Group). From there, I gradually expanded the program to more than ten cities in India and later to global locations such as Nigeria, Ghana, Dubai, Argentina, and Portugal. This helped me establish a network of potential community leaders who were passionate about building and growing Web3 communities in their own regions.

## Refining and Improving Community Building Strategies through Evaluation and Adaptation

As we continued to expand our community-building efforts, we encountered a number of obstacles, such as language barriers and difficulties in effectively connecting with communities in foreign regions. Additionally, there were instances where we required more support and coordination from local community leaders to ensure that our plans were executed smoothly.

To overcome these challenges, we began to focus on setting more specific and achievable goals for our events and actively worked on improving our planning processes. This has led to the more efficient and effective execution of our community-building initiatives.

Currently, we are proud to have community leaders from 5 different countries and 15+ cities, and we have successfully established 21 chapters, hosted 60 meetups, and welcomed over 6000 attendees. These achievements are a testament to our ongoing efforts to refine and improve our strategies for building thriving and engaged communities.

## Learn, Sleep, Repeat: Continuous Improvement and Evolution of a Community Building Program

Continuous improvement and evolution are essential elements of any community-building program. As the world and technology change, so must our approach to building and engaging communities. The Meetup-in-a-box (MIAB) program, for example, has undergone a significant evolution since its inception.

When the program first began, our primary focus was on educating and evangelizing the web3 technology and its potential. As we engaged with the community and gained a better understanding of their needs and interests, we recognized the need to tailor our approach and content to better serve them.

To achieve this, we implemented a continuous improvement process that involved regularly gathering feedback from community members, analyzing the data, and making adjustments to the program accordingly. This process has allowed us to continuously improve the program and make it more engaging, relevant, and valuable for our community members.

Building a strong developer community is key to unlocking the potential of a technology or product. A thriving community can provide feedback, collaboration, and a source of inspiration for developers. It can also lead to increased adoption and growth for a technology or product. However, building and maintaining a strong developer community is not an easy task. It requires time, effort, and the right elements in place. In this blog, we have discussed the 10 key elements of building a developer community. These elements range from having clear goals and values, to fostering a culture of collaboration and inclusivity. By incorporating these elements into your community building strategy, you can set the foundation for a thriving and engaged developer community.

In conclusion, a strong developer community is essential for unlocking the potential of a technology or product. With the right elements in place, a developer community can drive growth, provide valuable feedback, and foster collaboration. By prioritizing these elements, technology companies and open-source projects can create a community that supports the development and success of their products.
